# Lesson 4: Using AI to learn, not to skip

**Core question: When does AI help my learning?**
Objective: **S4.1** — decide for four homework scenarios whether AI use helps learning or replaces thinking, and write two personal AI-use rules with reasons.
Handouts: [My AI-use rules](handouts/my-ai-use-rules.html) · [Project planner](handouts/project-planner.html) (final project briefing)

*Last reviewed: 2026-10-25*

---

## Hook — 5 min

**Teacher script prompt:** "Two students both ask a chatbot to explain photosynthesis. One reads the explanation, closes the chat, and redraws the process from memory. The other copies the explanation into the essay. Same tool, same text. What's the difference?"

Let them answer: **what happened in the student's head.** "That difference — in your head, not in the tool — is the only line this lesson cares about. By the end, you'll have written your own rules about it."

## Main activity — 25 min

### Part A: the four scenarios (10 min, small groups)

Groups decide for each scenario: **helps learning / replaces thinking / depends — on what?**

1. "Ask a chatbot to explain the causes of World War I *in simple words*, then write your own summary and check it against the textbook."
2. "Paste the essay title into a chatbot and hand it the essay as your own."
3. "Do the practice exam first, *then* ask a chatbot to explain every question you got wrong."
4. "Stuck on the third maths problem for 15 minutes — ask a chatbot for a **hint about the method**, not the answer."

<details>
<summary>Teacher notes (not for students)</summary>
<p>1: helps — the chatbot did the <em>explaining</em>, the student did the <em>summarizing and checking</em> (the learning work). 2: replaces — nothing happened in the student's head; also an honesty problem, not just a learning problem. 3: helps — feedback on their own attempt is one of the strongest learning moves there is. 4: depends — the rule that makes it help: <em>try first, hints not answers, attempt again alone.</em> The scenario wording ("hint about the method") is designed to start exactly that rule conversation.</p>
</details>

Groups defend one "depends" verdict to the class. The class shapes the *condition* that flips it.

### Part B: write your rules (15 min, individual)

Each student completes the [My AI-use rules](handouts/my-ai-use-rules.html) handout:

- **Rule 1 and Rule 2** — one *process* rule (how I use it), one *boundary* rule (where I stop). Each with a one-line reason.
- Sentence frames on the handout for support; free writing for stretch.
- Optional: pairs swap rules and stress-test one ("what happens to your rule when the exam is tomorrow?").

**Frame for the room:** these are *their* rules — the quality bar is the reason, not the strictness. A lax rule with an honest reason beats a strict rule copied from the board. (See the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.html) on keeping the discussion judgment-free.)

### Final project briefing (remaining time)

Hand out the [project planner](handouts/project-planner.html) and walk through the [final project](final-project.html): a "chatbot survival guide" that applies all four lessons. Point out where each lesson feeds it (prediction → Lesson 1, fact-check habit → Lesson 2, fairness → Lesson 3, use rules → Lesson 4).

## Discussion — 10 min

- "When does AI help learning?" → When it explains, gives feedback, or unblocks you — and **you** still do the summarizing, checking, retrying.
- "When does it replace thinking?" → When it produces the *product* your teacher is assessing — the essay, the answers, the code — without your head touching it.
- "What did Lesson 1 have to do with this?" → If the chatbot only predicts likely text, then "ask the chatbot" is never a shortcut to *understanding* — the understanding happens in you or not at all. Connect back to the board list from Lesson 1.

## Exit ticket — 5 min

Hand in the rules sheet (or photograph it). Objective met = two rules with reasons that distinguish *product* from *process*.

## No devices

The lesson is unplugged by design — no AI use is required in class. All materials are on paper.

## Differentiation

**Support:** sentence frames on the handout ("I will use AI to ___, because ___. I will not use AI for ___, because ___."); two rules minimum.
**Stretch:** write the exam-eve version of your rules and defend the difference; or draft the *teacher's* version of acceptable AI use for this class and argue it.
