# Workplace track

Four self-paced modules, about 20 minutes each. The examples follow **Northwind Logistics**, a fictional logistics company with 1,200 employees in Germany and the Netherlands that is rolling out a generative AI assistant for customer service, operations planning and HR administration.

| # | Module | Core question | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [What AI is (and isn't)](module-1-what-ai-is.html) | Where is AI already in my work? | ~20 min |
| 2 | [Checking outputs](module-2-checking-outputs.html) | Can I trust this answer? | ~20 min |
| 3 | [Data and privacy](module-3-data-and-privacy.html) | What can I paste in? | ~20 min |
| 4 | [Oversight and escalation](module-4-oversight-and-escalation.html) | When should a human decide? | ~20 min |

Take the modules in order the first time. After that, use the one-page job aid at the moment of need:

**[Verify before you send — job aid (1 page)](job-aid-verify-before-you-send.html)**

Then check your judgment with the [scenario quiz](../assessment/scenario-quiz/index.html) (12 items, feedback on every option) and try the [performance task](../assessment/performance-task.html): correct a flawed AI-drafted memo and log your verification steps.

## Why these four modules

The design is based on a needs analysis (see [needs analysis](../design/needs-analysis.html)): most staff already use AI daily, but few have a routine for checking outputs, classifying data, or escalating decisions that affect people. Compliance alone is not the goal — safe, correct use in real tasks is. Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires organizations to take measures to support AI literacy; these modules are designed to support such measures, not to certify anyone.
