**B1.** A teammate shows you an AI answer about a supplier dispute and says: "It sounds completely sure, so it's probably right." The best response is:

- **a)** "Confidence in the wording doesn't tell us anything about truth — what does it claim that we can check?"
- **b)** "If it sounds sure, it's usually right."
- **c)** "Only trust answers about topics the AI knows well."
- **d)** "Sureness means it found the answer in a source."

**B2.** An AI-drafted claim note says: "The customer confirmed receipt on 3 March and accepted a €40 goodwill voucher, which has been issued." You sent no such voucher and have no confirmation record. Before sending this note you must:

- **a)** Fix the grammar and send.
- **b)** Verify each stated action and date in the systems; correct or remove everything you cannot confirm, then send an accurate version.
- **c)** Add "according to our assistant" and send.
- **d)** Ask the assistant whether its statement is true.

**B3.** An AI summary of a policy includes "as required by the EU Courier Safety Act 2024". You don't recognize the law. You should:

- **a)** Keep it — EU acts are easy to verify later by readers.
- **b)** Ask the assistant for the act's official number and keep that.
- **c)** Search for the act yourself; if it doesn't exist or doesn't say this, remove the reference and the claim it supports.
- **d)** Replace it with a different, real-sounding EU act.

**B4.** Which prompt follows the data rule taught in this program?

- **a)** "Here is the applicant's CV — score her suitability 1–100."
- **b)** "Here is the customer's email with his address and card digits — draft a refund reply."
- **c)** "Summarize this public news article about port strikes for our team update."
- **d)** "Here is our unannounced 2027 price list — draft a client email" (pasted into your personal chatbot account).

**B5.** While drafting, you pasted a customer's complaint text (identifying her family situation) into the assistant before realizing the rule. The chat is still open. Your first move:

- **a)** Close the chat quickly and carry on — no one checks drafts.
- **b)** Screenshot it and keep it in case anyone asks.
- **c)** Stop using that chat and report it the same day, so your lead and the data protection officer can decide the next steps.
- **d)** Edit the message in the chat to remove the details.

**B6.** Ops leadership wants the AI to auto-approve only "safe-looking" damage claims under €100, with no human look at all. The taught judgment says:

- **a)** Fine — small amounts don't affect people much.
- **b)** Not fine: claim decisions change something for a person, so a named human owns decisions; AI may prepare, summarize, and flag — the approval step stays human, with bias and error checks.
- **c)** Fine, as long as customers can email afterwards.
- **d)** Fine if the AI explains each refusal politely.
